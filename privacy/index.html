1. Introduction

This Privacy Policy explains how Sentinel ("Sentinel," "we," "us," or "our") collects, uses, discloses, and protects information when you use our mobile application and related services (collectively, the "App"). Sentinel is a hunting and hiking safety application designed for use in New Zealand, providing features including location tracking, zone and trail mapping, safety check-ins, SOS alerts, hunting and hiking data, and AI-powered tools.

We are committed to protecting your privacy and handling your personal information responsibly. This Policy is written to comply with New Zealand's Privacy Act 2020, the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and UK GDPR, the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA/CPRA), Apple's App Store guidelines, and Google Play's Data Safety requirements. If you are located outside New Zealand, additional rights described in Section 11 may apply to you.

By downloading, accessing, or using Sentinel, you agree to the collection and use of information in accordance with this Policy. If you do not agree with this Policy, please do not use the App.

2. Information We Collect

We collect the following categories of information:

2.1 Information You Provide Directly

  • Account information: email address, password (encrypted), your full name (kept private and never shown to other users), a nickname, and any profile details you choose to add. Your nickname is shown to other Sentinel users in safety alerts, for example if you send an SOS or your hunt block or route overlaps theirs. Use a nickname you are comfortable with others seeing; it does not need to be your real name.
  • Emergency contact: if you add an emergency contact, their name or phone number is stored in your account so the SOS and trip plan features can prepare a message to them. It is only visible to you. Messages to your contact are sent from your own phone, not through our servers.
  • Content you create: hunt logs, sightings, kill records, hazard reports, field journal notes and photos, zone boundaries, waypoints, trip plans, and messages sent to AI features (such as Sentinel Companion).
  • Thermals reports: if you choose to send them, your answers about what the air or your scent did (Section 5A).
  • Saved rifles and loads: rifles and loads you set up in the Ballistics Calculator are stored privately on our server, visible only to you, and deleted when you delete your account.
  • Condition profiles ("My Conditions"): you can save condition profiles — your preferences for wind, pressure, temperature, rain, cloud, moon and similar factors — and attach them to your hunt blocks so Sentinel can alert you when the conditions match. Condition profiles are stored privately on our server, visible only to you, never shared with other users or used for anyone else's alerts, and deleted when you delete your account.
  • Saved for later: you can draw a hunt block or a hike and save it without choosing dates, to use another time. A shape saved this way is stored privately in your account and is visible only to you. It is never used for overlap checks, safety alerts or the heatmaps, and it is deleted when you delete your account. When you decide to use it, you add dates and it becomes a normal hunt block or hike at that point. You can also turn a saved item into a Quick Block or Quick Hike, which lets you receive wind alerts for it. Those alerts are for you only; the item is still never shown to other users, and is still never used for overlap checks, safety alerts or the heatmaps.
  • Reports and blocks: if you report content or another user, we collect the report — the reporter, the user or content reported, the content itself, and the reason you give. If you block another user, we store that choice on your block list. This information is used only for moderation and safety (see Section 3) and is never shared with other users.
  • Communications: feedback, support requests, and survey responses you send us.
  • Payment information: when you purchase a subscription or in-app item, payment is processed by Apple App Store, Google Play, or RevenueCat. We do not directly collect or store your full payment card details; we receive confirmation of purchase and entitlement status only.

2.2 Location Information

Location data is central to Sentinel's safety features, and we want to be especially clear about how it works:

  • Precise location (GPS): We collect your precise real-time location when you use mapping, tracking, zone detection, hiker check-in, SOS, and related features. Background location — collected while the App is running in the background, if you grant "Always Allow" / background location permission — is used only while you are actively recording a track, so that your route continues to be recorded when the App is not open. Background location collection stops when you stop recording.
  • Recorded tracks and waypoints: routes, hunt zones, walking tracks, and waypoints you record or draw are stored against your account.
  • Sightings: when you log an animal sighting, its precise location is sent to our server, which blurs (fuzzes) it before it is stored. Other users may see that a species has been sighted in a general area. They never see who reported it or the exact spot.
  • Conditions recorded with your kills and sightings: when you log a kill or a sighting, the App records the weather and sky at that time — pressure, temperature, wind, cloud, rain, moon phase, and time relative to sunrise and sunset — together with an approximate location (rounded to about 1 km). This conditions record is visible only to you, is never shown to other users, and is not used in the heatmaps. It is deleted when you delete the kill or sighting it belongs to, or when you delete your account. We record it only to help you understand your own hunting.
  • Hazard reports: hazards you report are shown to other users on the map, without your name or nickname.
  • Weather and UV: to provide forecasts, your approximate location (rounded to an area of about 10 km) is sent to our server, which fetches and caches weather for that area. Our weather provider receives the area, not your identity.
  • Weather history: so we can work out the conditions attached to your condition profiles, kills and sightings, our server keeps short-term weather readings (for up to 10 days) for areas of about 10 km. These readings describe the weather in an area and are not linked to any person.
  • Track popularity: Pro users can see which DOC tracks are popular nearby, based on how many different users have logged completing each track in the last 30 days. A track is only shown when at least three different users have completed it. Your own GPS routes are never shown to other users.
  • Popular areas and species heatmaps: Sentinel shows anonymous heatmaps of where hunting activity has happened and where species have been reported. When you create a hunt block or log a kill or sighting, an anonymous record of the 300-metre map squares it covers is added to the store that powers these maps. This record does not contain your name, nickname or account. Instead it holds a one-way keyed tag worked out from your account using a secret key held securely on our servers that the App and our public data interface cannot read, so the record cannot be traced back to you. A square is only ever shown on a heatmap when at least three different people have contributed to it. See Section 6 for how long these anonymous records are kept and how deleting your block, kill or account removes your contribution.
  • Offline saving: if you create a waypoint while you have no signal, it is stored on your phone and uploaded to your account automatically once you are back online. Until it uploads, it stays on your device only.
  • Ballistics Calculator — "Fill from my location": if you tap "Fill from my location" in the Ballistics Calculator, your location is used for a one-off weather and elevation lookup to fill in the conditions for your shot. It is not stored.
  • Approximate location of a Thermals report: if you choose to send a report, a place rounded to the centre of a square about 2 km wide, and numbers about the ground at your spot such as height, slope and valley shape (Section 5A). We do not store your exact position, but the ground numbers could in principle indicate roughly where you were.
  • On-device copies for offline use: the App keeps a copy of your own blocks, planned hikes and Quick items on your phone so they are available when you are offline. Other users' data is never stored on your phone. These copies are cleared when you sign out.
  • Offline map downloads: map areas you download for offline use are stored on your phone only.

We do not currently derive approximate location from your IP address or network information for any purpose.

You can control location permissions at any time through your device's operating system settings (iOS Settings > Privacy > Location Services; Android Settings > Apps > Permissions > Location). Disabling location access will prevent core safety features — including SOS, zone detection, hiker check-in, and live tracking — from functioning correctly. We do not recommend disabling location access while you are out in the field, as this may compromise your safety.

Sharing location with other people. Some safety features share your location with other people by design:

  • SOS: other Sentinel users whose active hunt block or hiking route is within about 5 km of your SOS location receive your nickname and your location (as a grid reference and on the map), so they can call 111 for you. Your emergency contacts receive your location by SMS if you choose to send it.
  • Hiker check-ins: while you are checked in, hunters nearby see only your nickname and your check-in times, so they know people are in the area. They never see your route or your location.
  • Hunt block and route overlaps: when your hunt block overlaps another user's, both of you are warned. You each see the other's nickname (never your real name), the overlapping hunt block on the map, the relevant dates and times, and the block's hunt type and party size. Where a hiker is checked in through a hunter's block, the hunter and the hiker each see only the other's nickname and the check-in times — the hiker's route and location are never shown.
  • Hunting planned near your hike: if a hunt block is planned near a hike you have planned, we may alert you so you know to take care. The alert tells you only the block's dates — never the hunter's name or nickname, or any other detail about them.
  • Party / "Where's My Mate": party members you invite can see your location while sharing is on.

We do not make your live location publicly visible to other app users outside these features.

Community and research location data — not yet active. Sentinel includes a "Contribute to Research" setting (see Section 4.3) that will, in future, share aggregated or de-identified sighting and activity data with conservation research bodies. As of the date of this Policy, this feature has not been activated: no data is currently being transmitted to any external research or government body, regardless of your setting. We will update this Policy before any such sharing begins.

2.3 Photos, Camera, and Media

If you use Species ID, sightings, kill records or the Field Journal, we access your camera and, where you choose, your photo library.

  • Species identification and verification photos (sightings, kills and Species ID) are sent to our AI provider, Anthropic, to identify the animal and check the photo is genuine (for example, not a photo of a screen or print). Before sending, the App removes location and other metadata from the photo and reduces its size. These photos are not stored by Sentinel. They stay on your device; we keep only the result, such as the species identified and a confidence level.
  • Saving to your gallery: photos taken with the App's camera for sightings and kills are saved to your device's photo gallery once the record is saved, so you keep a copy. The first time, the App asks for permission to add photos. On iOS this is add-only access. On Android 13 and newer no permission is needed. On Android 12 and older, the system storage permission required to save photos also allows reading your photo library; the App does not read or upload photos from your library except ones you choose. If you decline, your record still saves without a gallery copy.
  • Field Journal photos you choose to save are stored in your account and can only be viewed by you.

2.4 Device, Usage, and Diagnostic Information

  • Device model, operating system and version, unique device identifiers, app version.
  • App and model version, sent with a Thermals report.
  • Crash and error diagnostics: if the App crashes or encounters an error, technical diagnostic information (such as stack traces and device state at the time of the error) is sent to our crash-reporting provider, Sentry, to help us identify and fix bugs. We configure Sentry to avoid capturing your IP address, GPS coordinates, and other personal content in these reports. See Section 6 for how long this data is retained.

We do not currently operate a general usage-analytics system (such as tracking every screen you view or every feature you tap) beyond the crash/error diagnostics described above and the AI-credit usage records described in Section 5.

2.5 Biometric Authentication

Sentinel no longer syncs health or fitness data. We have removed the Google Health Connect integration, and the App no longer reads your heart rate or step count. Any heart rate or step data that was saved to your own past track records before this feature was removed stays with your account, protected by the access controls described in Section 7, and remains visible only to you. You can remove it at any time by deleting the track it belongs to, or by deleting your account.

If you enable biometric login (fingerprint or face recognition), authentication is handled entirely by your device's operating system (Apple Face ID/Touch ID, Android biometric APIs). Sentinel does not receive, store, or have access to your biometric data itself — only a confirmation that authentication succeeded or failed.

3. How We Use Your Information

We use the information we collect to:

  • Provide core App functionality: mapping, zone detection, tracking, SOS alerts, hiker check-ins, and safety notifications.
  • Send you safety alerts — for example about severe weather (using MetService warnings), DOC closures, and hunting planned near a hike you have planned — based on your upcoming hunt blocks and planned hikes. Where an alert concerns a hunt block planned near your hike, you are told only the block's dates, never who the hunter is.
  • Alert you, through your condition profiles ("My Conditions"), when the weather and sky at your hunt blocks match the conditions you have chosen to watch for.
  • Operate AI-powered features (Sentinel Companion, Hunt Report, Safety Assessment, Hunt Debrief, Gear Recommender, Trip Planner, Species ID, and photo verification for sightings and kills), as described in Section 5.
  • Check and improve the accuracy of Thermals, using the reports you choose to send (Section 5A).
  • Maintain your account, process subscriptions and purchases, and manage the in-app credit system.
  • Verify sightings, kills and hikes, and award in-app credits and Pro time for them.
  • Prevent abuse, such as rate-limiting requests and reversing credits from refunded purchases.
  • Review reports, moderate user content, and apply the blocks you set, to keep the community safe. Blocking never hides overlap warnings or SOS alerts. It hides the blocked person's name, and the hazard reports and sightings they post.
  • Award badges, track milestones, and manage gamified features.
  • Improve, troubleshoot, and secure the App, including crash reporting.
  • Display non-personalized advertising to free-tier users, as described in Section 2.6.
  • Communicate with you about your account, safety-related notices, and (where you have consented) product updates.
  • Comply with legal obligations and respond to lawful requests from authorities.

We do not use your personal information to make automated decisions that produce legal or similarly significant effects on you without human involvement.

5. AI Features and Your Data

Sentinel provides several AI-assisted features (Sentinel Companion, Hunt Report, Safety Assessment, Hunt Debrief, Gear Recommender, Trip Planner, Species ID, and photo verification for sightings and kills). To provide these features:

  • We ask for your consent before you first use an AI feature, and only send your information to Anthropic once you have agreed. If you decline, the rest of the App keeps working, but features that rely on AI — including photo verification for sightings — will not run, so sightings cannot be verified or earn free Sentinel Pro time. You can withdraw your consent at any time in Profile > Privacy settings, which stops AI features until you allow them again.
  • Relevant information you provide (such as chat messages, hunt details, location and weather context, or photos for species identification) is sent to our AI infrastructure provider, Anthropic, for processing.
  • We do not use your individual, identifiable conversations or submissions to train Anthropic's underlying AI models, and our agreements with Anthropic restrict the use of API data for that purpose.
  • Sentinel Companion conversations are not stored on our servers once a session ends; each conversation is processed and relayed without being saved.
  • Generated Hunt Reports, Safety Assessments, and Gear Recommendations are cached for efficiency and to avoid redundant processing, linked to the relevant zone, track, or query. These cached outputs are automatically deleted after 90 days — see Section 6.
  • Photos for species identification and verification are only sent when you take or choose them for that purpose. Location metadata is removed first, and the photos are not stored on our servers (see Section 2.3).
  • AI outputs are generated by a machine-learning model and may occasionally be inaccurate or incomplete. AI-generated safety assessments, hunt debriefs, gear recommendations, and trip plans are provided for informational purposes only and should not be relied upon as a substitute for your own judgment, official safety advice, or emergency services.
  • Thermals reports are not sent to Anthropic or used by AI features.

5A. Thermals Reports (optional)

Thermals shows where the air and your scent are likely to flow. It is an estimate made from terrain, sun and forecast weather, not a measurement. If you choose, you can tell Sentinel what the air or your scent actually did, so the developer can compare the estimate with real days and improve it. This is called a Thermals report.

It is optional and off until you turn it on. The first time you tap "Report what the air did", Sentinel shows a screen that explains this, and nothing is collected unless you tap "Allow reports". This is a separate choice from AI features (Section 5) and from "Contribute to Research" (Section 4.3). You can turn it off at any time in Profile, Account, Privacy, under "Thermals reports". It is available to people with access to Thermals (Sentinel Pro, a free trial or earned Pro time).

What we collect when you send a report

  • Your answers: which way the air or scent went (for example uphill, downhill, up or down the valley, with the wind, swirled around, stayed put, or not sure); how sure you are; and, only if you add them, a compass direction, how you knew, the wind you felt and where it came from, the kind of spot you were in, and the cover (bush, scrub or open). If you tap "An animal winded me", that is recorded too. There is no free-text field, so we do not collect anything you type.
  • The time you give for what happened (rounded down to 15 minutes). You can report on anything in the last 24 hours.
  • An approximate place. Your phone sends a point, and our server rounds it to the centre of a square about 2 km wide before anything is stored. We do not store your exact position, a track, your accuracy, your altitude, or any device identifier with a report.
  • Numbers about the ground at your actual spot, as Sentinel's Thermals model used them: the height of the ground, its slope and which way it faces, the shape and size of the valley or gully (depth, length, catchment), whether cold air tends to pool there, how much sun the slope has had, and the sun's height and direction. They are stored for that spot and the ground cells around it (each about 30 to 90 m). They are measurements of the terrain, not coordinates, and no latitude or longitude is stored. Because New Zealand's elevation data is public, they could in principle be used to work out roughly where on the ground you were. We do not do this; they are used to re-run the Thermals model on that ground.
  • A record of what Thermals showed you: the type of air flow it estimated, its direction and strength, the forecast wind, cloud and temperature it used, and the version of the Thermals model. This record contains no coordinates and no full dates.
  • The forecast weather already saved for that area (about 10 km across) from 24 hours before to 3 hours after the time you give. Each saved reading is kept with how many minutes it is from your report, not with a clock time.
  • The version of the app and of the Thermals model you were using.
  • A quality score (0 to 1) that we work out from how sure you said you were, how you knew, and whether weather had been saved for that area. It is used to weight your report when it is studied.
  • Your consent record: that you said yes, when, which version of this section you saw, and when you turned it off if you did.
  • A count of how many reports your account has sent each day, to apply the limits below.

Checks we make on your own blocks. To stop false or bulk reports, a report is only accepted if the point your phone sends is within 2 km of one of your own blocks (and, for a dated block, within a day of its dates). We check this against your own blocks only. We do not look at anyone else's blocks or hikes, and the result of the check is not stored. Hikes do not count.

Limits. You can send up to 12 reports a day and 150 in 30 days, and one report for the same square and 15 minutes. The daily counts are kept for about 40 days and are not reset if you delete your reports.

Who sees it, and what it is used for. Only the developer of Sentinel can read reports. They are used to check how well Thermals matches what hunters find, and to adjust the model offline. They are not shown to other hunters or hikers, not shown on any map or heatmap, not used for advertising, not sold, and not shared with any third party. Our hosting provider stores them on our behalf under Section 4.1. The app shows you how many reports you have sent. The weather we store comes from a third-party weather provider and is kept only as part of the report record; it is not made available to anyone.

How long we keep it. A report stays linked to your account for 24 months, with the full ground numbers. After 24 months we remove your account link, the approximate place and the exact time, and we cut the ground numbers down to a rough summary: a slope band (0 to 10, 10 to 20, 20 to 30 or over 30 degrees), the compass direction the slope faces (one of eight), and whether it is a valley, a deep valley or a cold-air pool. The detailed numbers (height, the surrounding ground cells, exact slope, valley size, heating and sun readings) are deleted. We keep the answers, the record of what Thermals showed, the version of the Thermals model, the saved weather, a regional (about 10 km) area code, the month, and the time of day (in UTC). From that point it is no longer linked to you or your account, so we cannot show it to you or delete it for you, and it may be kept indefinitely as de-identified research data (Section 6). If you delete your account, every report still linked to it is deleted with it.

Your choices, at any time

  • Turn reports off in Profile, Account, Privacy. This stops new reports straight away. The app asks whether to also delete the reports you have already sent. If you choose to keep them, they stay linked to your account until the 24 month point above, and you can still delete them whenever you like.
  • "Delete my reports" in the same place deletes every report linked to your account, whether or not reports are on. This does not depend on having Pro.
  • Reports you have written but not yet sent (because you had no signal) are stored on your phone only and are removed from your phone when you turn reports off, delete your reports or delete your account.
  • Turning reports off does not affect any other part of Sentinel.

If you are outside New Zealand, Section 11 explains the rights you have. For reports, our legal basis (where one is needed) is your consent, which you can withdraw as described above.

6. Data Retention

We retain personal information for as long as your account is active or as needed to provide the App's services. Specifically:

  • Account and profile data: retained until you delete your account.
  • Hunt blocks and their recorded tracks: an automatic daily job removes a hunt block once it has ended more than 45 days ago (free accounts) or more than 6 months ago (Pro and Pro Max accounts), so old blocks stop cluttering your history. The GPS track recorded for that block is removed on the same basis, measured from when the track ended. Blocks you have added to Saved Blocks never expire, and a block that has not yet ended is never removed this way.
  • Your own kills, sightings, field notes and photos are kept when their hunt block expires — the automatic job does not delete them. They stay in your history, marked to show the block has been removed, until you delete them or delete your account.
  • Other tracks, hikes, and waypoints (together with any heart rate or step data saved to a track before that feature was removed — see Section 2.5): a track you record that was never tied to a hunt block, a hike check-in, and your waypoints are not removed by the automatic expiry job. They are retained until you delete them or delete your account, unless retention is required for safety, legal, or dispute-resolution purposes. Hikes you have added to Saved Hikes never expire.
  • Anonymous heatmap records: the anonymous records that power the popular-areas and species heatmaps (see Section 2.2) remain after your hunt block or activity expires, or after you remove it from your own history, so the maps keep working. These records hold a one-way keyed tag, not your name or account, and a square is only shown when at least three different people have contributed to it. Deleting your own hunt block, or a kill, removes that item's contribution from the heatmaps; deleting your account removes all of your contributions. A square still only appears when at least three different people remain.
  • Shapes saved for later (hunt blocks or hikes saved without dates, including any you have turned into Quick Blocks or Quick Hikes): stored privately in your account until you delete them or delete your account — see Section 2.1.
  • Saved rifles and loads (Ballistics Calculator): stored privately in your account until you delete them or delete your account — see Section 2.1.
  • Condition profiles ("My Conditions"): stored privately in your account until you delete them or delete your account — see Section 2.1.
  • Conditions recorded with your kills and sightings: kept with the kill or sighting they belong to, and visible only to you. They are deleted when you delete that kill or sighting, or when you delete your account — see Section 2.2.
  • Thermals reports: linked to your account for 24 months, then your account link, approximate place and exact time are removed, the ground numbers are cut down to a rough summary, and only de-identified answers and weather are kept. Deleted with your account, or earlier if you delete them (Section 5A). Daily report counts: about 40 days.
  • Weather history: short-term readings stored by area for up to 10 days, not linked to any person, and used only to work out the conditions described above — see Section 2.2.
  • Reports and block lists: retained while needed for moderation and safety, and deleted when you delete your account, unless retention is required for safety, legal, or dispute-resolution purposes.
  • Sentinel Companion conversations: not stored server-side at all — see Section 5.
  • Cached AI outputs (Hunt Reports, Safety Assessments, Gear Recommendations): automatically deleted after 90 days.
  • Species ID, sighting and kill photos: not stored on our servers. Only the identification result is kept, with the record it belongs to.
  • Field Journal photos: retained until you delete them or your account.
  • Weather and UV cache: stored by area only, not linked to you, and replaced as forecasts refresh.
  • Crash and error diagnostics (Sentry): retained for approximately 30 days.
  • Research data: not currently collected — see Section 4.3.

When you delete your account, we will delete or de-identify your personal information within a reasonable period, except where we are required to retain it by law or for legitimate business purposes such as fraud prevention or dispute resolution.
